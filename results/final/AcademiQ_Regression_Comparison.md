## 1. Academic Understanding: Multi-Metric Regression Benchmarking

### The Importance of Comparative Error and Goodness-of-Fit Visualizations
Evaluating continuous regression models requires visualizing multiple complementary metrics simultaneously:
1. **Goodness-of-Fit ($R^2$):** Measures the proportion of dependent variable variance explained by the model ($R^2 = 1 - \frac{SS_{res}}{SS_{tot}}$). A taller bar indicates superior variance explanation.
2. **Mean Absolute Error (MAE):** Measures average absolute deviation in the original scale ($	ext{Exam\_Score}$ points), offering robust interpretability resilient to isolated outliers. A shorter bar indicates higher precision.
3. **Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE):** Penalizes larger prediction mistakes more heavily due to the quadratic loss function. The spread between RMSE and MAE illustrates error variance.

Comparing these metrics across increasing model complexities allows researchers to visually diagnose **underfitting**, **optimal fit**, and **overfitting**.

## 3. Significance in the Complete Project & Viva Defense

### Why This Visualization Matters
This artifact provides visual proof of the project's core regression hypothesis: academic performance is fundamentally multifactorial. Looking at study hours alone leaves over $76\%$ of score variance unexplained. Expanding to all 19 factors elevates explained variance to $77\%$, while adding non-linear interaction terms yields zero practical benefit.

### Viva Defense Insight
- **Question:** *"Why does Polynomial Regression have a slightly worse MAE ($0.4682$) than Multiple Linear Regression ($0.4498$) despite having higher theoretical capacity?"*
- **Defense Answer:** Expanding 32 one-hot/scaled features to degree-2 polynomial and interaction terms inflates the feature space to 561 dimensions. While polynomial features can fit nuances in the training data, on unseen test data the unregularized interaction terms introduce slight variance error, confirming that linear feature addition perfectly captures the domain structure.
